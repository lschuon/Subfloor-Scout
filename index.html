From three links to a client-ready artist report

Scout takes an artist's Spotify, Instagram and TikTok links, pulls streaming and social data from three services, cleans it, scores the artist, and writes a report page. This prototype replays the real run behind a 30-artist, 91-page report.

Every number on this page comes from that run. Pick any of the 30 artists and press Run to watch each stage produce its part of the report.

The engagement math

Engagement rate is interactions per post divided by followers, or by views. Followers say how loyal the audience is; views say how well a post lands with whoever saw it. The fields start with the selected artist's average TikTok post.

Stage 5, up close

What the checks caught

Raw data from these services is messy. These are real problems found during the run, and the rule each one added to the system.

Porter MartinOld pinned posts inflate averages

Pinned 2024 posts were counted as recent. Rule: use only the 20 most recent posts and skip pinned ones.

Lennon VanderDoesA big TikTok can be dormant

Most of the last 20 videos were old. Rule: flag a TikTok as inactive with fewer than 3 posts in 180 days.

Instagram, several artistsHidden like counts read as zero

Some posts hide likes and report 1-3. Rule: leave those posts out of like-based rates.

BrayTwo artists on one Spotify page

His Spotify page is merged with a hip-hop act. Rule: flag the profile and do not judge the numbers until it is split.

Harter BrothersA spike too clean to trust

Listeners went from 66 to 12.9K in one week, almost all in Central Europe. Rule: unexplained spikes are flagged and kept off the cover.

Amelie Lucille, Schaefer LlanaWritten claims drift from the data

Drafts said 70% and 90% US audiences; the data said 59% and 95%. Rule: every figure in the text is checked against the data files.

All 91 pagesText that runs off the page

A headless browser measures every page against the footer before the PDF is made. Rule: no PDF until zero pages overflow.

ChartmetricRate limits leave gaps

The service hit its daily limit mid-run, so some age and gender data is missing. Rule: empty rows and columns are removed, never filled.

Under the hood

Six small programs, run in order

norm.py→metrics.py→copy_*.py→build.py→check_overflow.sh→make_pdf.sh

norm.py merges the three services into one record per artist. metrics.py computes engagement and the score. The copy files hold the written analysis. build.py lays out the HTML pages, and headless Chrome checks the layout and prints the PDF. Claude ran the data collection through each service's connector and wrote the analysis, with a person reviewing every page.
